# The order to teach, and why

1. ×11 and ×12 first: only "add the neighbour" (and "double"), so the right-to-left habit and carrying are learned on easy steps.
2. ×6 and ×7: add "half the neighbour" and "plus 5 if odd", the two ideas the rest build on.
3. ×5: the same ideas with nothing else in the way.
4. ×9 and ×8: the "subtract from 10, then from 9" pattern, and the special first and last steps.
5. ×4 and ×3: everything at once (subtract, half, odd, double).
6. The general two-finger method for any two numbers (section 4).
7. Checking by digit sums (section 5), taught once they can multiply, so they can check themselves.
A learner is ready for the next rule after about three right answers in a row on the current one; a slip on the odd-digit "+5" or
on the carry is the most common one, so practise those deliberately (problems with odd digits and large neighbours).
