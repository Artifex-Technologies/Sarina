# What the Trachtenberg method is

A system of fast mental arithmetic worked out by Jakow Trachtenberg, an engineer who, imprisoned in Nazi concentration camps,
kept his mind going by building it; it was published after the war (The Trachtenberg Speed System of Basic Mathematics, 1960).
Its idea: instead of memorising times tables, learn one short rule per multiplier that turns each digit and its right-hand
"neighbour" into one digit of the answer, written right to left, so the answer appears digit by digit with no intermediate rows.
It also has a general "two-finger" method for any two numbers and a quick check by digit sums. It suits people who like patterns;
it is a skill built by practice, a rule at a time, not a trick learned in one sitting.
